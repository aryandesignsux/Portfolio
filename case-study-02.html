Improving Loan & CC Management System UX on Olyv

Olyv Plus helps users track their complete credit profile, including loans and credit cards. The section was already there, but the drop-offs couldn't be ignored. A few iterations, field testing, and several espresso shots later, we found the version that made sense to our users.

Overview

What is Olyv Plus?

Olyv Plus is a ₹39 monthly recurring plan that helps you track everything related to your credit profile, be it your credit score or your existing loans and credit cards.

Paan shopkeeper

Took a loan for inventory and wanted to check his EMI due date. Got confused by the sheer number of categories.

Security guard

Had a personal loan from Bajaj Finance, saw the Home Loan category and tapped it — thinking he might get a home loan from Olyv.

Fruit vendor

Doesn't know what category his loan falls into. He only knows he took it from Ujjivan SF Bank.

Dairy owner

Took a personal loan for renovation of his village home. His bureau report shows an auto loan. A case of credit fraud.

Restaurant manager

Wants to track and pay his credit card bills.

Salaried worker

Just wants to keep track of his loan EMI payments regularly.

The core problem: Showing every category, including ones that users didn't have an ongoing loan in, only deviated the user's intent. Users were made to read through categories and consciously decide which one to go into, leading to a lot of misclicks.

Research

How do I validate my hypothesis?

Method 01
User testing on ground

We walked up to users matching Olyv's persona and asked them to navigate the flow, to validate where we were going wrong. We went to paan shops, general stores, and mobile shops, and also talked to quite a few quick delivery gig workers to know how and what they understood about their credit score, its profile, and how they used the Olyv Plus feature.

We recorded a few sessions on our devices so we could revisit them and find insights around what to build for them.

Design decisions

What I changed, and why

Decision 01
Active Loans & Credit Cards highlighted separately on the homepage.
Why the old way failed

Categories with no ongoing loans were also shown.

What we changed

Active Loans and Credit Cards are shown separately, clarifying user intent on the homepage itself.

Decision 02
Showing Bank Name & Loan Amount first.
Why the old way failed

Cognitive load on the user to filter their loan by category first, instead of showing the loan upfront.

What we changed

Every loan is now led by bank name and loan amount, reducing confusion and thinking from the user's end.

Decision 03
Closed Loans kept in a separate section altogether.
Why the old way failed

No clear demarcation between active and closed loans. Users thought a closed loan was still active.

What we changed

A separate tab for closed loans, to avoid this confusion altogether.
